## Pattern Observations

1. **Compliance-AI is the densest, most receptive vertical.** Ten of 24 targets are RegTech/compliance. Q1 2026 alone saw ~$3B into RegTech ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/04/16/nine-figure-raises-dominate-in-powerful-q1-for-regtech-deals/)). These companies sell to compliance officers who cannot free-trial their way to a decision — the LLM research phase effectively IS the funnel — and the sellers' regulated-industry credibility lands as peer language, not agency-speak.

2. **Comparison-war categories are the fastest closes.** Numeral-vs-Anrok, Truewind-vs-Basis, Delve-vs-Vanta, Karomia-vs-Atlas Metrics: wherever both sides already publish "X alternatives" pages, the marketing lead instantly understands "same war, new battlefield (ChatGPT)." No education needed — the free snapshot converts a known anxiety into a line item.

3. **The accounting/CFO stack combines money + urgency + AI-literate buyers.** Record capital into CFO tools (Sifted: $66.5M EU record) plus buyers (accountants, CFOs) who are themselves heavy LLM users — they viscerally believe "buyers research via ChatGPT" because they do it. Truewind, Stacks, Basis, Abacum, Numeral all fit.

4. **Fresh raises (≤90 days) beat big raises.** Hadrius (Jul 2026), Diligent AI (Mar 2026), Duna (Feb 2026), HR Duo (Jun 2026) just received explicit growth mandates but haven't hired agencies; the $8-25K/mo category agencies target the Series B+ names (Abacum, WithCoverage, Anrok) — leaving the $1.5-6K entry offer uncontested at seed/A.

5. **Two undervalued wedge types emerged:** (a) *identity crises* — Diligent AI's name collision and Delve's scandal are provable, screenshot-able AI-answer problems, the strongest cold-open imaginable; (b) *unowned new categories* — Antidote's "billing compliance," Duna's "business identity network," Pace's "insurance ops agents" have no incumbent answer in LLMs yet, so a small content sprint can own 100% of answer-share, which makes the sprint upsell demonstrably measurable.
